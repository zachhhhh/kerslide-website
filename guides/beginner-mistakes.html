8 beginner mistakes and how to fix them

Habits from other block puzzles can cost you in Kerslide. Here are the most common ones and what to do instead.

Kerslide looks like a familiar block puzzle, so new players often bring habits that work in other games and backfire here. These are the eight mistakes we see most, with the fix for each.

1. Ignoring where the board moves

In most block games a clear just frees space. In Kerslide the whole board moves one cell right after a row clear and one cell down after a column clear. Players who forget this finish a row and then discover their nearly complete column has been pushed next to the wrong blocks. Fix: before every clear, glance at the preview and ask where your other half-built lines will end up. The shift rule guide shows it with pictures.

2. Building in the top-left corner

It feels natural to start in the top-left. But that is the corner the board moves away from, which makes it the best reserve space on the board. Fix: place non-clearing pieces toward the right and bottom, and keep the top-left open for long pieces.

3. Treating the edges as walls

The right and bottom edges are exits. A block on the last column leaves the board with the next row clear. Fix: when you have stray blocks along an edge, plan a clear in the matching direction and let the shift remove them for free.

4. Placing the easy piece first

Small pieces fit almost anywhere, large ones do not. Placing two small pieces first can leave nowhere for the big corner. Fix: find a spot for your hardest piece before placing the others.

5. Letting the combo drop

Your combo multiplier survives two placements that clear nothing, and resets on the third. Players often lose a combo they could have kept with one small clear. Fix: count your quiet moves. See the exact numbers in scoring and combos.

6. Leaving one-cell holes

A lone empty cell can only be filled by the single-block piece, and that piece is dealt roughly once in 67 draws. Fix: prefer placements that leave gaps two or three cells long.

7. Chasing one huge clear

Setting up a four-line clear is tempting, but it fills the board while you wait. Steady single and double clears keep the board open, keep the combo alive and score well. Fix: take the clear in front of you unless the bigger one is one move away.

8. Rushing

There is no timer in Kerslide. Nothing is gained by placing quickly. Fix: drag a piece over two or three spots and watch the preview before letting go.

Controls you might have missed

  • Keyboard: keys 1 to 3 pick a piece, the arrow keys move it, Enter places it and Esc pauses.
  • Accessibility: the settings include colorblind-friendly shapes, a high-contrast mode, reduced motion and larger text.
